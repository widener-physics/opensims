River crossing

A boat crosses a river that is flowing to the right. The boat's speed and heading are set relative to the water; the water carries it downstream at the same time. Where it lands and how long it takes both come from adding two velocities. Aim straight across and you cross fastest; aim upstream and you land closer to the point opposite, but the crossing takes longer.

What to try. With the boat faster than the current, press "landing straight across" and check the heading against sin θ = c/vb; then compare its crossing time with the fastest crossing. Set the current faster than the boat: the "straight across" preset grays out because no heading can get there, and "least drift" finds the best you can do, sin θ = vb/c. Tick "view from the water": the boat's path becomes a straight line along its heading, and it is the banks that move.

Units: meters and seconds. The current is uniform across the river unless "fastest mid-river" is ticked, which uses a parabolic profile that is zero at the banks and c at the center (mean ⅔c); in that case the path curves and the water-frame view is not available, since different parts of the river move at different speeds.
